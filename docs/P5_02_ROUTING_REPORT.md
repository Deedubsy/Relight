## Implementation contracts recorded before coding

The start-unlocked underground endpoint and priority splitter belong to the exploration campaign; the legacy catalogue/starting state stays unchanged. One belt lane and one optional single-item inserter filter retain D-P5-3/5. No splitter item filter or new inserter tier is introduced.

Provisional task defaults: an underground endpoint costs 5 steel + 2 copper; a splitter costs 5 steel + 2 copper. Both draw no power, like belts. Underground endpoints occupy one tile each. A pair faces the same direction, with an input followed by an output one to five tile centres away (zero to four hidden tiles). Pairing uses the nearest same-facing endpoint; an intervening same-facing input/output prevents skipping it. Perpendicular tunnels may cross. Only endpoint footprints occupy the surface; intervening terrain/machines remain untouched. Pair placement is an atomic, paid, in-reach construction group with a span preview. Existing individual endpoints can be rebuilt through ordinary construction history.

Underground items travel at belt speed, in the input's saved item queue until transferred to the output. The queue uses belt spacing and a capacity proportional to the paired distance; an unpaired input can hold one tile's stock. A lost or rotated counterpart stops transfer and retains all queued items; packing an endpoint recovers its own queued stock. An endpoint holding items cannot rotate. Reconnection resumes held stock without creating or destroying it.

A splitter occupies two tiles across the direction of travel: 2×1 north/south and 1×2 east/west. The anchor is its top-left tile. It merges two rear ports into an eight-item FIFO queue and serves two forward ports at up to 15 items/s combined, subject to downstream belt capacity. Left/right are relative to its facing. Balanced output alternates successful deliveries; left/right priority tries that side first, immediately falling back if it cannot accept the leading item. With both outputs blocked, it keeps the item. Rotation validates the new footprint and reach atomically.

An inserter's optional filter is free to change through an ordinary command. It constrains the next pickup from a belt, routing buffer, assembler, chest or station arrivals. It never discards an already-held item, even if the filter changes during a swing. Normal destination acceptance and backpressure still apply. Settings, buffered items and construction history survive save/load and replay.

These mechanical defaults are implementation assumptions within the accepted scope, not owner-approved balance.

## Implemented controls and integration

U selects underground pairs: click an input, then the output. The line and endpoint ghosts show validity before the ordinary sim command commits. Escape cancels without charge. A matching existing input adopts its facing; the pair command can reuse matching endpoints, building and charging only missing endpoints in one construction history group. An intervening conflicting endpoint refuses the whole operation. This also permits reconnection after ordinary removal without requiring undo.

J selects the splitter. Derived width/height are shared by placement, reach, collision, walking, rotation, rendering and save validation. Legacy square machine fields and the legacy starting catalogue remain unchanged. Belts enter routing devices through their rear ports; inserters retain their ordinary source/target rules. An inserter can extract from the output endpoint or a splitter, but cannot reach into the buried input queue. Splitter extraction follows FIFO order.

E on an inserter or splitter opens its setting selector; T cycles the filter or output priority. The selected setting and sim-owned buffer/status descriptions are visible. Commands enforce reach, validation and conservation; the renderer does not mutate gameplay. Escape closes the settings panel and restores canvas focus, including at the narrow stacked layout. Optional settings and queues are saved; invalid modes, settings, buffers, footprints and occupancy are rejected. Construction history detects intervening configuration changes. Campaign evidence fingerprints and generated rules include routing defaults without altering legacy fingerprints.
