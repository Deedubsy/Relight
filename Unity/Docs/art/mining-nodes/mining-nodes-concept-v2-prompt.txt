Use case: stylized-concept.
Create ONE polished landscape art-direction comparison sheet for Relight, a top-down industrial survival/factory game. This is a sprite concept sheet for owner review, not a screenshot or finished sprite atlas.
Layout: clean 3 columns by 2 rows, six equally sized panels. Small restrained title "RELIGHT • RESOURCE NODES". Panel labels, in reading order, exactly: "IRON ORE", "COPPER ORE", "COAL", "STONE", "CRUDE OIL", "RUBBLE". No extra marketing text.
Within each panel: one large, very clearly readable resource cluster on muted olive game ground; below it, two much smaller thumbnail versions of that cluster, one on olive ground and one on warm concrete. Label the small strip "SCALE STUDY". Provide generous empty space around each silhouette. Do not fill the panels with landscape scenery.
Game ground palette: olive #52614b, muted factory concrete #7c7b68, subtle paving joints #4b5751. Keep backgrounds understated and nearly flat, so we judge sprites against the current game's actual palette. Thin dark dividers and cream labels, simple readable typography.
Sprite style: detailed hand-painted 2D game assets with crisp deliberate edges and bold simplified material planes, weathered and grounded industrial aesthetic. Overhead camera looking almost straight down with just enough visible rock side faces for depth, consistent across all six. Soft upper-left daylight, short lower-right contact shadows. Low exposed seams and scattered chunks, NOT tall mountains or inventory icons. No pedestals, tile-shaped bases, circular badges, glowing selection rims, UI frames around individual rocks, plants or characters. Irregular organically broken cluster boundaries with a few small edge fragments. High material differentiation at small size. Not photorealistic, not low-poly 3D, not blurry painterly concept art, not tiny retro pixel art.
IRON ORE: chunky angular blue-slate grey rocks, deep fractures, muted rusty red iron seams, a few cool metallic exposed faces. Predominantly slate with restrained rust.
COPPER ORE: rounded ochre/brown host rocks with strong orange copper veins and a few turquoise oxidation patches. Clearly copper-bearing stone, never coiled wire or finished copper plates.
COAL: low clusters of jagged charcoal-black chunks, broad restrained blue-grey highlights, fine dark coal dust. Distinct high-contrast fractured silhouette, not shiny obsidian gems.
STONE: flatter pale grey/beige slabs, limestone-like fractured faces, scattered light gravel. Visually paler, flatter and broader than iron ore, no metallic seams.
CRUDE OIL: a flat, irregular natural ground seep, cracked oil-darkened earth with small dark glossy puddles and a very subtle purple/teal iridescent sheen. No barrel, pumpjack, tap, machine or rock mountain; no lava or neon glow.
RUBBLE: broken concrete chunks with exposed rusty reinforcement bars, a few reddish mineral fragments and dusty debris. Reads as urban demolition rubble, not a stockpile of finished metal plates.
The six node types must share coherent lighting, perspective and rendering quality while remaining distinguishable by silhouette as well as colour. Allocate most of the sheet to the artwork.
CRITICAL CAMERA AND HEIGHT DIRECTION FOR THIS NEW SHEET: View all six subjects from an almost vertical 85-degree overhead camera. They are WALKABLE shallow deposits, mostly flat exposed mineral beds, not large boulder piles. At least 90% of visible rock surface should be top faces, with only a narrow side edge. IRON ORE is a fractured, nearly flush exposed slate seam broken into shallow angular plates with rusty red fissures. COPPER ORE is a shallow scattered bed of flattened brown mineral chunks with orange veins. COAL is a wide flat bed of low jagged black fragments, NO central peak. STONE is low pale flat slabs. RUBBLE is a thin scattered layer of broken concrete and bent rusty bars. OIL is completely flat. No upright boulders, no tall central rock, no mountains, no stacked heaps, no isometric camera. Short tight contact shadows only. Apply the same overhead angle and flattened construction to the small thumbnails. All material colours and label/layout instructions above remain unchanged.
